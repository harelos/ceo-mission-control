# CEO × Agent Company Operating System — Tiger Brands Global / NovaHair

**Created:** 9 September 2026  
**Purpose:** Preserve the operating model developed in the CEO/agent discussion and give future agents a single source of truth for how the company should work.

> The goal is not “more agents.” The goal is a company that keeps producing useful, verified work when the CEO is absent.

---

## 1. The CEO-level decision

The current bottleneck is not capability. It is **attention dependency**.

A founder can use very strong agents and still remain the operating system of the company if the agents wait for the founder to:
- decide what matters;
- select the next task;
- judge whether work is finished;
- connect departments;
- approve every low-risk action;
- translate reports into work.

The target operating model is different:

**CEO sets direction, resource boundaries, standards and exceptional decisions.**  
**Departments own outcomes.**  
**Managers decide the next permitted action.**  
**Workers produce artifacts or execute bounded playbooks.**  
**QA verifies against frozen standards.**  
**A deterministic queue/scheduler keeps work moving.**

The CEO should increasingly ask:

1. Is this actually a CEO decision?
2. If not, which department owns it?
3. Why did it reach the CEO?
4. What policy is missing so this class of problem does not need CEO attention again?

---

## 2. What the CEO should own

The CEO job is intentionally small:

1. **Direction** — what the company is trying to become and the current strategic priority.
2. **Capital/resource allocation** — where money, agent capacity, inventory and attention go.
3. **Standards** — what “good enough” and “unsafe” mean before the work begins.
4. **Outcome ownership** — who owns Growth, Revenue and Operations.
5. **Constraint removal** — cash, inventory, creative volume, supply, payment health, tracking or another bottleneck.

The CEO should not routinely choose individual ad creatives, edit SEO articles, debug routine tracking issues, upload products, or manually translate every report into tasks.

---

## 3. Recommended lean company structure

Do not build a giant artificial org chart. Start with **three outcome departments and five reusable role profiles**.

### Department A — Growth
**Mission:** Acquire customers profitably.

Owns:
- Meta acquisition;
- creative strategy and testing;
- competitor research;
- acquisition landing-page experiments;
- acquisition analytics;
- creative testing backlog.

Primary metrics:
- new-customer revenue;
- CAC / contribution after acquisition;
- creative hit rate;
- marginal efficiency;
- number of qualified challengers.

### Department B — Revenue
**Mission:** Make every visitor and customer worth more over time.

Owns:
- CRO;
- email/lifecycle;
- retention/reorders;
- upsells/cross-sells;
- SEO and organic growth;
- product merchandising and daily product uploads.

Primary metrics:
- store CVR;
- AOV;
- repeat rate;
- email revenue;
- organic qualified traffic and organic revenue;
- product/indexation quality.

### Department C — Operations
**Mission:** Keep promises reliably and profitably.

Owns:
- suppliers;
- inventory and stock risk;
- fulfillment exceptions;
- customer support;
- refunds/chargebacks;
- payment and contribution-data integrity.

Primary metrics:
- fulfillment time;
- stockout risk;
- refund/chargeback rate;
- unresolved customer incidents;
- gross/contribution margin integrity.

### Shared execution profiles

**Maker** — produces the requested artifact or scoped implementation.  
**Verifier** — independently checks the output against frozen acceptance criteria and evidence.

Five profiles do not mean five models thinking all day. They are reusable roles that wake only when work exists.

## 4. How managers should work

A department manager is not a permanent chat room. It should wake when something requires judgment:

- daily planning;
- a worker finishes;
- an exception occurs;
- daily close;
- a scheduled weekly review.

Everything else should be deterministic where possible.

### Manager responsibility

The manager asks:

- What is the largest constraint in my department right now?
- What is the highest expected-value eligible job in the queue?
- Can I handle it directly, delegate it, or should it be blocked?
- Did the output pass the standard?
- What did we learn that should update policy or backlog priority?
- Does anything actually require CEO judgment?

The manager should not consult another department casually. Cross-department consultation should be allowed only when the missing answer materially changes the work and cannot be resolved from shared state or policy.

Recommended default per root task:
- 1 initial attempt;
- 1 targeted repair;
- 1 consultation maximum;
- 1 model escalation maximum;
- all child work shares the root budget.

---

## 5. The company backlog

The future of the company cannot live only in the CEO's head or chat history.

Maintain a persistent ranked backlog:

- **P0 — Incident:** checkout, fulfillment, payment or serious customer failure.
- **P1 — Active growth constraint:** no creative pipeline, inventory risk, tracking break, weak offer/funnel.
- **P2 — Expected-value growth:** experiments, lifecycle, CRO, product merchandising.
- **P3 — Compounding:** SEO, catalog expansion, content refreshes, automation improvement.

When an agent finishes a job, it should take the next eligible job inside its authority instead of waiting for the CEO to invent another task.

## 6. What “good enough” means

Do not use “flawless” as the standard. It creates endless work and makes autonomy impossible.

A deliverable passes only when three gates are met:

### Gate 1 — Correct and permitted
- right product/account/currency/scope;
- facts and claims supported;
- no prohibited action;
- no invented evidence.

### Gate 2 — Functionally verified
- artifact opens or behavior works;
- relevant tests/checks pass;
- evidence is attached;
- known failure conditions are handled safely.

### Gate 3 — Commercially usable
- understandable;
- on-brand;
- suited to its intended channel;
- ready for the next business stage.

A creative may be **ready to test** without being “a winner.” A page may be **ready to publish** without being guaranteed to rank. Business outcome is measured later.

### Feedback categories
Every CEO correction should be classified as one of:
- critical defect;
- rule violation;
- preference;
- new request.

Then ask whether policy should change. This converts founder feedback into organizational memory.

## 7. Autonomy ladder

Do not jump from “agent recommends” to “agent can change anything.”

### Level 0 — Observer
Reads data and reports.

### Level 1 — Planner
Chooses next work, creates backlog, drafts artifacts and recommendations.

### Level 2 — Builder
Creates finished local/draft artifacts, verifies them and queues them for approval.

### Level 3 — Bounded operator
May execute exact low-risk playbooks under hard limits.

Examples:
- publish a page only after a fixed SEO QA gate;
- launch a creative only inside the approved testing campaign and budget envelope;
- send an approved service template for a verified scenario.

### Level 4 — Mature delegated authority
Only after demonstrated reliability, limited categories of capital allocation or operational writes can be delegated.

High-risk actions remain CEO-controlled unless separately authorized.

## 8. CEO reporting format

A CEO should not receive a wall of research. The CEO report should fit on one screen and answer:

- What changed?
- Why does it matter?
- What is being handled automatically?
- What is blocked?
- What decision is required from me?

### Example weekly CEO brief

**COMPANY**  
Revenue / contribution / cash or stock risk / vs target.

**GROWTH**  
What moved, biggest constraint, what the department already did.  
**CEO decision:** only if required.

**REVENUE / ORGANIC**  
Organic revenue, CVR/AOV, lifecycle recovery, SEO/product pipeline.  
**CEO decision:** only if required.

**OPERATIONS**  
Fulfillment, stock, refunds, payment/chargeback risk.  
**CEO decision:** only if required.

**AGENT COMPANY**  
Jobs completed, first-pass QA rate, auto-repair count, escalations, quota/usage and CEO interruptions.

**YOUR 1–3 DECISIONS THIS WEEK**  
Only the choices that truly require founder judgment.

## 9. CEO cadence

### Daily — about 15 minutes
Review:
- money;
- biggest growth movement;
- major risk;
- decisions waiting.

Approve/reject true CEO decisions and leave.

### Weekly — 60–90 minutes
Each department answers:
- What moved?
- Why?
- What did we learn?
- What is the current constraint?
- What will you do next without me?
- What requires my decision?

The CEO reallocates resources and updates policy.

### Monthly — 2–3 hours
Ask founder-level questions:
- Are we selling the right products?
- Are we in the right market?
- Where should the next unit of capital go?
- What capability should the company build?
- What should we stop doing entirely?

## 10. Agent-company health metrics

Do not judge autonomy by how many agent messages exist.

Track:

**First-pass acceptance rate**  
How often work passes QA without a repair.

**Autonomous resolution rate**  
Share of mature workflow jobs completed without CEO help.

**Escape rate**  
Bad work that reached production/customer-facing state.

**CEO interruption rate**  
How often the system needed the CEO for something that should have been below CEO level.

**Useful output per quota unit**  
Accepted work divided by actual measured subscription/API usage.

The desired direction is fewer CEO interruptions and more accepted work per unit of agent capacity.

---

## 11. Token / cost discipline

The company should be available 24/7, not reasoning 24/7.

Rules:
- use deterministic code for polling, arithmetic, deduplication and admission control;
- wake models only for tasks that need judgment or generation;
- keep global concurrency small during calibration;
- reserve capacity for the CEO's own coding usage;
- never silently fall back from subscription usage to paid API spend;
- separate subscription quota, billed API money and external business spend;
- UNKNOWN usage is not zero usage;
- manager/worker/reviewer work shares one root-task budget.

The first objective is to measure real capacity for 72 hours and then one full week before deciding how many simultaneous departments the current plan can support.

## 12. Foundry-specific notes to verify

The earlier repository audit found two important implementation details in the inspected `foundry-app` main branch that should be verified against the actual running installation:

1. the inspected Codex completion path recorded zero input/output/cost fields in its local history, which means the local ledger may not reflect real Codex subscription usage;
2. the inspected load path disables active autonomous loops after restart, so durable unattended operation needs explicit persistent work-order recovery rather than assuming loops survive a process restart.

These are not reasons to rebuild Foundry. They are reasons to verify usage telemetry and durable execution before trusting it with a week of unattended work.

## 13. The Meta Ads Manager model

“Upload this creative” is not a CEO decision.

The Meta Manager's mandate should be:

> Own profitable customer acquisition through Meta. Protect proven winners, maintain a healthy challenger pipeline, choose the next test, create or delegate required creative, QA it, measure it, update the backlog and escalate only decisions outside delegated authority.

The manager should not ask the CEO “what should we test today?” when policy and data can answer it.

The desired flow is:

**data → diagnosis → hypothesis → asset → QA → eligible execution → measurement → learning → next task**

The CEO receives the result, not every internal step.

See `META_MANAGER_OPERATING_POLICY_V1.md` for a draft operating contract.

## 14. The SEO / Organic Growth model

SEO should not be “an article agent plus a product uploader.” It should be an outcome system with the mandate:

> Increase qualified organic traffic and organic revenue from all commercially relevant Tiger Brands products.

It owns:
- product/brand/query discovery;
- content and product-page production;
- collection/comparison/FAQ opportunities;
- internal linking and technical publish checks;
- daily eligible product uploads;
- indexation/ranking/CTR monitoring;
- revenue and conversion measurement;
- refresh/consolidation of weak pages.

The CEO report should not say “32 articles published.” It should say what happened to organic visibility, commercial sessions, revenue, rankings, product indexation and the next allocation decision.

See `SEO_ORGANIC_GROWTH_POLICY_V1.md`.

## 15. The seven-day test

The target is not a philosophical “autonomous company.” Run a measurable test:

Imagine the CEO disappears for seven days.

A successful system should return with evidence similar to:
- SEO pages/products published under policy;
- new creative concepts produced and QA'd;
- eligible low-risk tests handled under delegated limits;
- customer/fulfillment exceptions resolved under playbook;
- rejected work repaired or blocked safely;
- one short list of CEO decisions, rather than a week of waiting.

The real success metric is:

> **The business moved forward, quality was checked, resource use was recorded, and the CEO did not have to supply the next instruction.**

## 16. Immediate implementation order

1. Verify the actual Foundry runtime, auth mode, usage telemetry and restart behavior.
2. Implement one durable work-order state machine rather than more agent profiles.
3. Create the three department charters and shared backlog.
4. Freeze QA rubrics and escalation rules.
5. Give Growth one real autonomous pilot workflow.
6. Run the 72-hour usage/reliability calibration.
7. Expand only after accepted-output and CEO-interruption metrics support it.

---

## Core principle

**Do not delegate isolated decisions. Delegate the system that repeatedly makes those decisions inside a safe policy.**

That is the shift from founder + smart assistants to CEO + operating company.
